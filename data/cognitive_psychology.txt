Cognitive psychology is the scientific study of human mental processes, investigating how people perceive, think, remember, learn, solve problems, and direct attention. It treats the human mind as a complex information processing system, bridging behavioral observations with underlying neurological mechanisms.

Human perception involves interpreting sensory inputs received from the physical environment. Sensory organs convert light, sound, pressure, and chemical signals into neural impulses that the brain organizes into meaningful cognitive representations. Top-down processing utilizes prior knowledge and expectations, while bottom-up processing constructs perceptions directly from sensory data.

Memory systems are traditionally divided into distinct stages: sensory memory, working memory, and long-term memory. Working memory temporarily holds and manipulates information required for complex reasoning and comprehension, but possesses limited storage capacity. Long-term memory stores episodic life experiences, semantic factual knowledge, and procedural motor skills across extended durations.

Decision-making and problem-solving processes are heavily influenced by cognitive heuristics and biases. Heuristics are mental shortcuts that allow individuals to make rapid evaluations under uncertainty, but they frequently lead to systematic cognitive errors. Common phenomena include confirmation bias, availability heuristics, and framing effects that distort rational probabilistic judgments.

Neuroplasticity demonstrates that the human brain continuously reorganizes neural pathways in response to learning, novel environmental stimuli, and neurological injury. Synaptic connections strengthen through repeated activation, providing the physiological foundation for lifelong skill acquisition, behavioral adaptation, and cognitive rehabilitation.
Executive functions represent high-order cognitive control processes mediated primarily by prefrontal cortex neural circuits, enabling goal-directed behavior, flexible problem solving, and adaptive self-regulation. Core executive functions comprise cognitive flexibility, which permits switching between competing task demands; inhibitory control, which suppresses impulsive automatic behavioral responses; and working memory updating, which actively monitors and replaces obsolete task-relevant information. Dysregulation of executive functioning characterizes numerous neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions, including attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder, frontotemporal dementia, and traumatic brain injury.

Language acquisition and psycholinguistics explore how the human mind acquires, structures, and comprehends complex symbolic communication systems. Theoretical models contrast B.F. Skinner behavioral operant conditioning with Noam Chomsky nativist hypothesis of a universal grammar and innate language acquisition device. Psycholinguistic research investigates the mental lexicon, speech segmentation, syntactic parsing models, and the dual-stream neurobiological model of language processing, tracing auditory comprehension through the ventral stream and speech motor articulation through the dorsal stream connecting Wernicke and Broca cortical areas.

Attention mechanisms govern how cognitive processing resources are selectively allocated amid overwhelming sensory information streams. Donald Broadbent early filter bottleneck model proposed that unattended sensory inputs are filtered out early in processing, whereas Anne Treisman attenuation model suggested that unattended signals are weakened rather than completely blocked. Posner spatial orienting paradigm distinguishes between endogenous voluntary top-down visual attention driven by internal intentions and exogenous reflexive bottom-up attention captured by sudden novel external environmental stimuli.

Consciousness studies and metacognition investigate subjective experiential awareness and the capacity of individuals to monitor, evaluate, and regulate their own cognitive states. Global Workspace Theory posits that conscious awareness arises when information is broadcast across a distributed network of long-range cortical neurons, making it globally accessible to diverse cognitive subsystems. Metacognitive monitoring, encompassing feeling-of-knowing judgments and calibration of confidence ratings, governs how learners allocate study time, revise erroneous beliefs, and navigate complex intellectual reasoning under epistemic uncertainty.
